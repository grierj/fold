# Accounts and household help

Sign in with your email address. If you and your spouse share one email, one of you creates the account and adds the other under My household. When you sign up for an event, choose everyone who is coming.

Only the person whose account it is gets the emails. If you'd like your own reminders, or you lead a group, set up your own email address. A free one is fine. Ask the church office to help move you to your own account. Text messages are planned for later; Fold does not send SMS yet.

Parents: add children under 13 to your household. Teens aged 13 to 17 can get their own account with approval and consent. Add your teen to your household and sign them up yourself while the church office checks the guardian link. Your household keeps managing sign-ups until the new account is ready.

Teens with their own account need account consent before using Fold. An
admin can record a parent or guardian's consent with evidence from a signed
form or another checked source. Account, SMS, and photo consent are
separate. Revoking account consent signs the teen out and keeps the account
history. An admin's suspension stays in place even if consent is granted
again.

## Share an email

One email address belongs to one account with one person's name and password. Add everyone else who shares it to My household on your Profile page. Choose Child, Teen, or Adult, and add their birth date. An adult needs a birth date before you can sign them up. An admin must check the guardian link for a child or teen before you can sign them up.

Each person you choose by name has their own sign-up and uses one seat. Choose their answer and whether they attend in person or remotely. For a named child attending in person, you can request one childcare spot when the event offers childcare. Extra unnamed guests, such as +2, are separate. Use those only when the event allows guests, and do not count your named household people again as extra guests.

## Create a separate account

An adult in your household, or a church admin, can invite a household adult or eligible teen to use their own email. Teens need a birth date showing they are 13 to 17 and a confirmed household guardian link. Children cannot have accounts. If someone's saved type no longer matches their age, correct their details first.

1. Open Profile, then My household. Choose Create their own account beside the person.
2. Enter their own email address and choose Send account invitation. The email must have no existing account or open invitation.
3. They open the email link within 24 hours and choose a password. Their details and sign-ups stay as they are until an admin approves.
4. After an admin approves, every past and upcoming sign-up moves to their new account. Their answers and waitlist places stay the same. The new account stays in your household and appears under Accounts.

Until an admin approves an adult, nothing changes: your household keeps managing their sign-ups as before. You'll see Account waiting for an admin beside them. They get an email after approval and sign in with the password they chose. If the request is rejected or expires after 30 days, their details and sign-ups stay as they were, and you can invite them again.

For a teen, admin approval creates an account waiting for consent. A guardian already confirmed for the household person needs a newly confirmed guardian link to the teen account. Consent and this fresh check must both be complete before sign-ups move. The church office can also record consent evidence, but still checks the new guardian link.

While a teen account waits, keep managing their sign-ups through My household. Care notes and photo settings stay editable. Their name, type, and birth date stay fixed, and removal is unavailable; ask the church office for help with those changes. The account can sign in to check its waiting status. An approved account waiting for consent does not expire like an unanswered request.

Group memberships do not move to the new account. Some private or invite-only events may no longer be available. Ask the event's leader or the church office about access.

You can resend an invitation to give it a fresh 24 hours; the old link then stops working. Cancel it before choosing a different email. Changing the person's name, type, or birth date cancels their invitation. Removing them cancels it too. Editing care notes, photo permission, or the household's name or address keeps it open.

If a responsible account is disabled, ask your church office to check it before moving the sign-ups. If the invitation sender's account was disabled, ask for a fresh invitation after it is turned back on.

## Teen accounts and consent

Teens aged 13 to 17 need their own email and birth date to request an account or accept an ordinary account invitation. Children under 13 cannot create accounts. A parent or guardian can add them to My household instead.

A teen account starts waiting for consent. After you choose a password, you can sign in to see Waiting for consent, check your account status, or log out. Your church office can help with consent. Groups and events become available after consent is granted. A teen moving from your household keeps all sign-ups there until account approval, consent, and the new guardian check are complete.

Group leaders, event hosts, and admins see Teen and Child badges on the rosters they manage. Other members see minors in attendance and membership counts rather than as named roster entries. Teen accounts have no public profile picture.
