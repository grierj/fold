# M1.9 brief: households and household people

Source of truth: PLAN.md 5.3 (Household, HouseholdAccount, HouseholdPerson, GuardianLink, Registration), 5.10, section 6 (households rows), 10.3, DEC-8, DEC-23, DEC-32, and the M1.9 acceptance list. It builds on M1.5's locked registration path and M1.8's remote and childcare counting, so it lands after M1.8 (see the judgment-call log). It ships in three slices, one PR each.

## Slice 1: households and links (no registrations yet)

- Migration: `households(id, name, address, created_by, created_at)`; `household_accounts(household_id, user_id unique, relation guardian|adult|teen)`; `household_people(id, household_id, first_name, last_name, kind child|teen|adult, birth_date null, care_notes, photo_consent bool default false, created_by, converted_user_id null, deleted_at null)`; `guardian_links(id, guardian_user_id, teen_user_id null, person_id null, status pending|confirmed|rejected|revoked, confirmed_by null, check_note, created_at, decided_at)` with a CHECK that exactly one of `teen_user_id` and `person_id` is set.
- An account gets a household on first use ("My household" creates it, named after the account's last name; editable). An account belongs to at most one household.
- `GET/PUT /api/v1/me/household`, `POST /me/household/people`, `PUT/DELETE /me/household/people/:id` (account holder; any adult account in the household). Adding a `child` or `teen` person writes a pending `GuardianLink` and notifies admins (bell, `household.link_pending`, a waiting item). Adding an `adult` needs no approval.
- Admin: `GET /api/v1/admin/guardian-links?status=pending`, `POST /admin/guardian-links/:id/confirm` and `/reject` (a check note is required to confirm, DEC-32). Audited: `household.created`, `household.person_added`, `household.person_removed`, `guardian_link.confirmed|rejected|revoked`.
- Policy: `policy.CanManageHousehold`, `policy.CanSeeHouseholdPerson`, `policy.CanSeeCareNotes(viewer, child, event window)`; table tests for the section 6 household rows.
- UI: Profile → "My household" card (Illuminator-reviewed): list people with Child/Teen badges and "Waiting for an admin to confirm" until confirmed; add/edit form with first name, last name, kind, birth date (child and teen), care notes (child), photo consent. Admin → Approvals gains a "Household links" section.

## Slice 2: registering household people

- `registrations.household_person_id` (nullable) and `registered_by`; CHECK exactly one of `user_id`/`household_person_id`; partial unique index on `(occurrence_id, household_person_id)`.
- RSVP takes `people: [{personId, status, attendanceMode}]` alongside the holder's own answer; each named person is one registration and one seat (5.4), under the same occurrence lock, waitlisted in line order like anyone. Only confirmed people can be registered (pending child/teen links refuse with 409 "Waiting for an admin to confirm").
- Visibility of the event applies to the account holder (a household person sees nothing; they have no login). Messages and notices about a household person's registration go only to the holder (7.x recipient resolution).
- Attendee lists show "Mary Smith (via John Smith)", with Teen/Child badges; leaders see a household person's name and age only for their own events (section 6).
- Disabled holder (DEC-38): their household people's registrations are hidden and uncounted with theirs, and restored or left out the same way.
- Care notes: leaders/hosts of an event see a registered child's care notes from 24 h before to 24 h after the occurrence; guardians and admins always.

## Slice 3: turning a household person into an account, docs

- `POST /me/household/people/:id/convert {email}` (holder or admin) sends an invitation to the new, unique email. Accepting it creates the account, moves the person's registrations to it (same occurrence rows, `user_id` set, `household_person_id` cleared), sets `converted_user_id`, and adds a `HouseholdAccount` (relation adult or teen). A teen conversion keeps the guardian link pending admin confirmation and the teen account starts `pending_consent` until M1.10 consent exists (DEC-69's rule).
- `docs/MEMBERS.md` with the 5.10 member guidance, linked from the profile page's household card.
- Tests: policy table, API tests for every acceptance item, migration up/down.

Slice 2 implementation choices (October 22, 2026):

- A batch is all-or-nothing on validation or capacity refusal; successful answers may mix going and waitlisted people. An omitted holder answer and unmentioned people remain unchanged. Each person chooses their own attendance mode. Existing waiters get released resources before new batch requests.
- An adult household person needs a birth date proving age 18 today in the church's time zone. Children and teens need their own confirmed guardian link and a birth date. An admin cannot bypass these requirements.
- Named childcare is an explicit per-child `childcareRequested` choice, initially off, available only in person. It uses the same care pool and DEC-80 queue as unnamed requests. The holder's count and single Ages field describe other, unnamed children only.
- `registered_by` stays the original responsible adult account when another adult edits an answer. Household people never enter account or membership rosters. Notices go to that holder; event-wide notices deduplicate by holder.
- Attendee names and ages use the narrower household permission rule. Event care notes require a current going registration on a scheduled date; managers have the inclusive 24-hour window, while confirmed guardians and admins retain their profile access. Household GET omits inaccessible notes.
- Profile has one inline household name/address editor. The existing household remains visible when its last person is removed.
- Seat-only manager removal retains its existing promotion behavior for now. The separate re-add double-count cleanup is deferred. Slice 2 does not claim those behavior changes.
- Database concurrency and browser acceptance remain unverified until the checks can run against Postgres and Chromium; implementation alone does not tick acceptance boxes.
